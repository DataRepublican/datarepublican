What changed

It didn't happen by accident.

The drop starts the month the policies changed. Three actions signed on the first day:

  • Jan 20, 2025
    National emergency declared at the southern border.
    White House, "Declaring a National Emergency at the Southern Border"
  • Jan 20, 2025
    "Securing Our Borders" order: resume Remain in Mexico, end catch-and-release, build physical barriers.
    White House, "Securing Our Borders"
  • Jan 20, 2025
    The U.S. Refugee Admissions Program suspended pending review.
    Federal Register, "Realigning the United States Refugee Admissions Program"
  • Feb 2025 – Aug 2026
    19 straight months between 7,822 and 13,542 encounters.
    CBP Nationwide Encounters

The longer story

For 35 years the share kept climbing.

The share of U.S. residents born abroad rose from 7.9% in 1990 to 14.1% in 2020–24. The border numbers above show the first sharp break in the flow behind that line.

Foreign-born share of U.S. residents. 1990 and 2000: decennial census. 2009 onward: American Community Survey 5-year estimates, summed across all 3,144 counties. Census Bureau data, computed from bulk files.

These two charts measure different things. The border chart counts encounters at the border, not residents. The foreign-born line counts everyone living here who was born abroad, and most of them are citizens, green-card holders or visa holders. Census data also runs a few years behind: its latest window ends in 2024, so it doesn't show the change since 2025 yet.
★ ★ ★ ★ ★

Executive orders can be undone.

The border numbers changed because of executive action. This Congress followed through: it ended food-stamp eligibility for refugees and asylees in July 2025, ended their federal Medicaid eligibility as of October 1, and cut refugee funding 18% for 2026.

The White House asked for deeper cuts. Getting them takes more Republican votes, not fewer. That's on the ballot.
